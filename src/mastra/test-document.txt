Old Major is a prize boar and the oldest animal on Manor Farm. He is highly respected by all the other animals for his wisdom and experience. One night, Old Major gathers all the animals in the barn to share a dream he had about a future where animals live free from human oppression.

In his speech, Old Major describes the harsh reality of their lives under human control. He explains that humans exploit animals, taking everything they produce - eggs, milk, and even their lives - while giving back only the bare minimum needed for survival. He calls this system unfair and encourages the animals to rebel against their human masters.

Old Major teaches the animals a revolutionary song called "Beasts of England" which becomes their anthem of hope and freedom. The song describes a golden future where animals control their own destiny, free from human tyranny.

Three nights after sharing his vision, Old Major dies peacefully in his sleep. However, his ideas live on and inspire the other animals, particularly the pigs Napoleon and Snowball, who develop his teachings into a philosophy called Animalism. This philosophy becomes the foundation for the rebellion that eventually drives the humans from the farm.

Old Major's role in the story is that of the ideological founder of the revolution. Though he dies before seeing his dream realized, his vision of animal equality and freedom from oppression sets the entire plot in motion. He represents the idealist who plants the seeds of change but doesn't live to see the consequences of his ideas.
